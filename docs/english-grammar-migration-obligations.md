# Grammar migration obligations

This is the routing record for the grammar migration and lexical-analysis
rewrite, not a corpus measurement or a second task graph. The implementation tickets own these
outcomes; their `needs:` fields schedule the work. Entries retain source
examples and identity keys from the former tickets/fog without treating old
parser acceptance, census counts or discarded implementation recipes as
linguistic authority. Re-fetch exact supported Oracle text at implementation.

The 2026-09-07 lexical-analysis rewrite supersedes old ranking, unique-winner
and tie-error prescriptions in the historical evidence below. Retain every
valid grammatical analysis with exact source realization; neither a packed
forest nor an old selected tree licenses an ungrammatical reading. The new
implementation tickets determine carriers and proof boundaries.

### Coin and voting constructions across families

- `english-coin-flip-residue`: `english-v2-lexeme-owned-verb-frames` owns
  complete `come up heads`/`come up tails`, `whichever comes first` and
  `came under your control` frames, preserving the ordinary count noun `tail`
  separately from the result expression. An old closed scanner does not
  prohibit other independently licensed Word Forms. `english-v2-relative-clause`
  owns Goblin Assassin, Mana Clash and Rakdos the Showstopper's
  `each player whose coin comes up tails`, and Goblin Traprunner's
  `For each flip you win`. `english-v2-subordinate-clause` owns Edgar, King

  own Squee's Revenge's `If you win all the flips, draw two cards for each
  flip.` and the quantity in `Flip a coin that many times or until you lose
  a flip, whichever comes first.`; subordination/coordination own its tails.
  `Repeat this process until ...` and `You and target opponent each flip a
  coin.` go to context/coordination, not a dedicated coin-procedure parser.
- `english-voting-procedure`: `english-v2-subordinate-clause` owns
  `Starting with you, each player votes for <choices>` and preverbal

  `return or embark`, `Redhorn Pass or Mines of Moria`, and

  `an artifact, creature, or enchantment card in your graveyard` and
  `up to one creature`; a label reading cannot erase them. Travel Through

- `english-recovery-modern-gate`: retire the Modern-only scope and frozen
  census targets. `english-v2-grammar-migration-close` owns current supported
  Vintage verification, using existing subset tooling rather than a second
  format gate.
- `english-recovery-walker-derive`, `english-large-function-extraction`, and
  `constructions-crate-unification`: retire refactors of deletion-bound v1

  `english-v2-grammar-lexical-source`; consolidation of replaced v2 paths belongs
  to `english-v2-grammar-migration-close`. Deletion of v1 remains governed by
  [the original cutover plan](decisions/english-v2-rewrite.md#cutover-plan),
  not by the earlier long-tail handoff. Current consumers require replacements.
- `english-structural-recovery-long-tail`: retain the historical observation
  that 3,166 of 3,759 unresolved spans had unique text (2,858 of 3,366 faces)
  and 2,582 of those singleton spans had 11–40 words. The inference that this
  imposes a 508-face ceiling on grammatical family work is withdrawn: distinct
  sentences can share a missing construction or interaction. The obligation
  for `english-v2-grammar-migration-close` is to inspect composed structures,
  not equate repeated whole text with grammatical generality; the current
  long-tail owner is `english-v2-stage-5-grammar-buildout-14-10`. Historical
  all-format/Modern counts do not define today's supported corpus.
