# A bare relative clause opens with its subject

`BareRelative` accepts any finite Clause with an object Gap, including one
that begins with a preposed adjunct. In Spark Double (*… with an additional
+1/+1 counter on it if it's a creature, it enters with an additional loyalty
counter on it …*), *counter* gets a bare relative *[if it's a creature, it
enters _]*: the fronted *if*-clause opens the relative, and transitive
*enter* supplies the object Gap.

## Owner's ruling

A bare relative clause (no *that*, *which* or *who*) cannot open with a
preposed adjunct before its subject. CGEL: "Bare relatives always have the
subject in initial position" (Ch. 12 §3.5.5, p. 1055). The ruling cited
§3.2.2, which is the *the result of which* percolation type on p. 1040. The
passage is in §3.5.5, on p. 1055 as ruled.

## Change

- Require a bare relative's Clause to be subject-initial.
- Use a feature on the Clause, or exclude the preposing productions as its
  head:
  - `FrontAdverbial`;
  - `Adjunct:Clause+…/Before/…`;
  - `PreposedTemporalAdjunct`;
  - `PreposedPrepositionAndTemporal`.
- Name no word.
- *That* and *wh* relatives are unaffected; CGEL's own *that only the day
  before I had sent* keeps its Reading.
