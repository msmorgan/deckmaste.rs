# Give the description frame its relations and its player-sorted reads

One region: the relations a description can carry (otherness, control, ownership)
and the reads whose subject is a player rather than an object. They share the
condition and modifier rows, the possessor vocabulary, the demonstrative that
reads a subject back, and the same `ControlledBy`/`OwnedBy` neighbourhood, so a
round that touches one touches all of them.

## Otherness complement residues

The source-anchored other-marking was never open — `OtherThan This` has
elaborated since the anchored complement was minted with the source as its
intended anchor (finding 113), and the TARGETING row `Other` gives the refusal
it is supposed to give. The word's inventory is 3,143 occurrences over 2,955
cards, of which 1,897 over 1,832 are that cell (finding 770). These are the
measured leftovers, each a different question.

- **"The other [X]"**, 104 occurrences over 102 cards — finding 111's subset
  complement, still needing a cardinality no binding records. The real gap here,
  restated because a sweep of the word will keep finding it.
- **The anaphoric "another of those"**, 4 occurrences over 4 cards
  (Clockspinning's "put another of those counters on it"): a PARTITIVE over a
  group mention rather than a complement over a description. No row, and under
  finding 85's bar on its own; it belongs with `SomeOf`'s family if anything
  ever schedules that.
- **"Another card named [X]"**, 12 over 12, the Grandeur cycle's discard cost.
  The complement composes — what these want is the cost shape around it, so they
  are a cost-line question and not an otherness one.
- **The distributive MILL**, found while pricing Altar of the Brood as a witness
  ("Whenever another permanent you control enters, each opponent mills a card").
  Every benched mill has a singular subject whose library `They` can read; a
  distributive subject has none, so the clause cannot be written. Not measured
  beyond the one card — **count the family before scheduling it.**
- **What the class-word witnesses still wait on**, so nobody re-prices them as
  otherness gaps: the trigger event's amount readback is built (`eventAfter`
  announces `outcomeB DamageDealt` at both damage events) and the Screaming
  Nemesis trigger is benched as a FRAGMENT — its second sentence wants three
  unbuilt things (a "dealt damage this way" container over the trigger's own
  event, a standing prohibition on gaining life, and a rest-of-the-game
  duration `Duration` has no row for), which are the conditional-container and
  span rounds' work, not this one. Of the eleven supported cards writing this
  reading, the other ten stop on an Adventure face, two Sieges, a reflexive
  trigger, [CR#615.13]'s prevention trigger, a power-up cost or a granted quoted
  ability — none on otherness and none on the magnitude. Pain for All's SECOND
  line ("Whenever enchanted creature is dealt damage, it deals that much damage
  to each opponent") has both halves benched; its FIRST line is what the card
  still waits on.

## The control tests the description rows refuse

Three measured shapes are all a control test the current rows cannot describe:
over a definite description, distributed over an `Each` agent's members, and as
an independent counted clause. They share the condition and modifier rows and
the demonstrative that reads a subject back.

### The DEFINITE under a control test — 6 supported lines

"draw a card if you control the creature with the greatest power or tied for the
greatest power" — Abzan Beastmaster, Padeem Consul of Innovation, Summon: Fenrir,
Thickest in the Thicket, Triumph of Cruelty, Triumph of Ferocity.

Chapter sixty-eight's `Definite` announces its referent, and `Matches` demands a
`Bindingless` subject because a condition introduces nothing — so the phrase can
only be written there by losing the mention silently, and the refusal is the
honest form of that (probed). `Exists` is wrong in the other direction: it IS the
indefinite. What the corpus writes is a control test over a definite description,
and neither condition row says it.

### The distributive subject read back — 16 supported modifier lines

"Each opponent sacrifices a creature with the greatest power among creatures THAT
PLAYER controls", plus whatever else writes the shape. `ControlledBy (That
PlayerW)` under an `Each` agent does not elaborate, and the superlative is not
why — checked with the modifier removed. The demonstrative reads a DISTRIBUTED
mention, a different question from the singular reads `That` was built for. It
costs Crackling Doom, Soul Shatter's family and Summon: Valefor. The singular
twin ("target player sacrifices … creatures THEY control") is writable and
benched (Consume), so it must not regress.

### The conditional threshold over a possessor set — ~9 lines

"unless your opponents control eight or more lands" and its family, 2.3% of the
"your opponents control" mass. An INDEPENDENT clause, not a relative one, so it
needs a counted condition over a described set rather than a possessor.

## Ownership as a possessor axis, and the plural relational possessor

Ownership is missing twice: as a relation that DESCRIBES an object, and as a
possessor derived member-wise from a group. Exile is what forces the axis —
[CR#109.4] leaves an exiled card no controller, and [CR#108.3]'s owner is the
only possessor left — so the descriptive gap and the axis gap are one row. The
missing `OwnedBy` predicate beside `ControlledBy` is the same gap the ownership
condition below runs into from the condition side.

### The ownership relation as a description — 6 supported lines

"cards your opponents own", all six naming cards in EXILE. This is the
descriptive twin of chapter fifty-four's possessive-zone reader: that one widened
`OwnedBy`, which writes "your opponents' graveyards", where this one needs an
`OwnedBy` PREDICATE beside `ControlledBy`.

Object ownership as a possessor axis distinct from `ControlledBy` — necessary on
the same exile grounds — is not a second entry: it is the same row, and the two
land together or not at all.

### The plural RELATIONAL possessor

"their owners' hands", "its controller's graveyard" pluralised: a possessive over
a relational noun derived from a GROUP, which `ControllerOf`/`OwnerOf` cannot
mint because they derive one referent from one object (`badGroupOwner`). Distinct
from the row above — the possessor here is not a named set but a relation applied
member-wise.

## The ownership predicate and the per-game history reads

The condition vocabulary reads control but not ownership, and the landed
per-game lookback has no carrier because every one of its cards is stopped by a
second, differently-shaped read. Both are reads over history and possession in
the same region, and both arrive with a named blocker that a round must state
rather than discover.

Authority: [The kind index joins; union marking is spelling](../../decisions/kind-index-joins-union-marking-is-spelling.md)
— The Fallen's relative clause heads a joined kind; read it as a reader over the
joined kind, not as a marked union reader.

### The ownership predicate — 5 cards, and a second blocker that eats them all

Five supported cards write "you both own and control [X] and a creature named
[Y]": Gisela, the Broken Blade; Graf Rats; Hanweir Battlements; Vanille,
Cheerful l'Cie; Titania, Voice of Gaea. The grammar has `ControlledBy` with no
owner counterpart, so the landed conjunction row cannot reach them — "both own
and control X" is itself two conditions and one of them has no row.
[CR#108.3] is the ownership rule.

**All five lines end in "exile them, then meld them into [Z]", and MELD has no
vocabulary in this grammar.** A round that buys ownership alone unblocks none of
these five cards. It must either say so in its own record, or find the ownership
carriers that do not meld.

### The per-game lookback's remaining carriers

`Lookback.ThisGame` is landed and ungated. 28 supported lines write "this game"
and none was benched when the residue was measured, every carrier having a
second blocker. Approach of the Second Sun is off the list — it is benched whole
and is this row's first witness. The rest:

- the 21 commander-cast counts — blocked on cast-zone provenance, not this
  round's;
- **Gollum, Obsessed Stalker** — "each opponent dealt combat damage this game by
  a creature named Gollum, Obsessed Stalker loses life equal to the amount of
  life you gained this turn". Subject, complement, window and name are all
  writable (the shape is benched at Wicked Akuba in miniature); what refuses is
  the `EventSum` magnitude in the body;
- **Yidaro, Wandering Monster** — "if you've cycled a card named Yidaro,
  Wandering Monster four or more times this game": there is no `EventName` for a
  CYCLING. The keyword-action event name is the same gap as the per-turn state
  read ("as long as you've surveilled this turn", Darkblade Agent, Eye of
  Duskmantle), so the two want one answer;
- **The Fallen** — "each opponent and planeswalker it has dealt damage to this
  game": a relative clause with BOTH an overt subject ("it") and its head in the
  complement position, which is neither `Happened` (no head) nor `HappenedTo`
  (no subject). A third reader shape, over a joined-kind head;
- **Frodo, Adventurous Hobbit** and **Frodo, Sauron's Bane** — "if the Ring has
  tempted you two/four or more times this game": no Ring machinery anywhere, so
  these are out of reach until that exists.

Once Upon a Time's "the first spell you've cast this game" is an ordinal
identity read and belongs to the ordinal-word round, not here.

### Measured zeros to preserve

"Chosen/noted this game" is **zero** lines. Every "first/second time" in the
corpus is **turn-scoped**, never game-scoped.

## "One of your opponents": spelling of an indefinite, or its own row

The partitive widening was refused, leaving one question. 32 lines write "one of
your opponents", and the phrase's covariance with the clause's subject is nearly
total, so it is very probably a SPELLING of `Indefinite Opponent` — "an
opponent" where the subject is you-anchored, "one of your opponents" where it is
not. Three lines are on the wrong side of that covariance and they are what
stops the round.

Authority for treating a phrasing fact as spelling rather than as a constructor:
[The kind index joins; union marking is spelling](../../decisions/kind-index-joins-union-marking-is-spelling.md).

### The measurement (finding 547)

Covariance one way 15/15 and 8/8; the other way 3/3 and 80/83. The three
exceptions are Fiendish Duo, Gisela, Blade of Goldnight and Rem Karolus,
Stalwart Slayer: all three write "an opponent" under a bare "a source" / "a
spell" in a REPLACEMENT static.

### The decision the round owes

Whether the replacement frame is a SECOND ENVIRONMENT with its own rule — in
which case the spelling stands and the renderer owes it — or a GENUINE CROSSING,
in which case the determiner is a row after all. Decide it; do not re-open the
partitive widening, which was already refused.

**RULED (user, 2026-08-27): single environment.** Neither fork. There is no
second spelling environment for replacement statics, and no determiner row: the
covariance rule is the only rule, and "one of your opponents" is a spelling of
`Indefinite Opponent`. The three violating lines (Fiendish Duo, Gisela, Rem
Karolus) are exceptional card templating, owned by an eventual RON macro that
respells the same concept — the macro layer absorbs the exception, the grammar
does not. The round builds nothing for the three lines beyond recording them as
macro-owed; the 80/83 spelling read proceeds on the measured covariance.

### Fixed either way

`groupMention (PlayerGroup _)` stays False. Its own reader measured 8 lines
against `Each Opponent`'s mass and that measurement is not disturbed by this
decision.

## Player-sorted predicates and life reads

Four measured gaps sit at the same place: a predicate or a read whose subject is
a **player**, where the object-sorted twin is already written. Two are
predicates, two are life-total reads, and all four surface in the same region of
the description frame.

Authority:
[The kind index joins; union marking is spelling](../../decisions/kind-index-joins-union-marking-is-spelling.md)
— the player-sorted half of an object-sorted row is a question about the kind
index, judged by lowerability and algebra, not an occasion to mint a second
marked row beside the first.

### The player-headed comparison predicate — 4 cards

"target opponent who has more life than you do" (Keeper of the Flame, Keeper of
the Light, Oath of Mages, Namor). `Superlative` is a player-kind qualifier and is
**not** this: it bounds a player's number against a fold of the player's own
*set*, where these bound it against an independent standard written beside it.
That is `Compare`'s question, and `Compare` is `Predicate bs Object` over a
`Characteristic` — so the gap is exactly the player-sorted half of that row. The
condition frame (`CompareAmt` over `PlayerStatOf`) already says the same thing
about a player; the description frame cannot.

The two Keepers additionally want the as-you-activate targeting restriction, for
which this vocabulary has nothing.

### The negated existential over players — 5 supported lines

"if no opponent has more life than that player", the Commander-creature grant
family. A quantified player **subject** under a negation, which is neither
`PlayerGroup`'s plural read nor `NotCond`'s negation of a whole condition.

### The point-in-time snapshot rider — 1 card

"life equal to that player's life total **as the turn began**" (Sengir, the Dark
Baron), and the shape of any read taken at a past moment rather than now. Absent
from **both** layers — core's `Noted` and `EventSum` are different mechanisms —
and it is the only thing between Sengir and a bench line; the loss trigger and
the life read both landed.

### The life exchange — 13 lines

"exchange life totals with target opponent", defined by [CR#701.12c,701.12g].
A two-player effect whose operand is the set operation this grammar already has
(`LifeOp.Set`), so what is missing is the **two-participant clause**, not the
arithmetic. It is also what blocks two of the six supported "difference between"
lines, which otherwise wait on their own surface.
